POLLING RATE

8000Hz: Real Gain or Big Number?

Polling rate is how often your mouse reports its position. 1000Hz = every 1ms; 8000Hz = every 0.125ms. The jump is real, but you'll only feel it on a 240Hz-or-higher monitor, where the cursor gets drawn often enough to show the difference.

The cost: 8K polling eats CPU and battery. On a mid-range PC, 2000–4000Hz is the sweet spot most players can't distinguish from 8000.

SENSORS

DPI Is Not Accuracy

DPI (really CPI) is just how far the cursor travels per inch — a sensitivity setting, not a precision score. A 26,000 DPI sensor set to 800 plays exactly like a 3,200 DPI sensor set to 800.

What actually matters: tracking speed (IPS), acceleration tolerance, and zero smoothing or angle-snapping. Buy those, not the biggest DPI number on the box.

KEYBOARDS

Rapid Trigger, Explained in 30 Seconds

Magnetic (Hall-effect) switches measure how far a key is pressed, instead of just on/off. Rapid trigger resets the key the instant you start releasing it — reset points as low as 0.1mm.

Why FPS players care: strafing and counter-strafing get snappier because the key stops when your finger does, not when it passes a fixed point. It's the biggest keyboard shift since mechanical switches went mainstream.

SURFACES

Speed vs Control: It's About Friction

Every pad has two frictions: static (force to start moving) and dynamic (resistance while gliding). Speed pads keep both low — great for tracking heroes and wide flicks. Control pads raise dynamic friction so the mouse stops exactly where you tell it — the tactical-shooter preference.

Humidity changes friction too, which is why a pad can feel "different" on a rainy day. That's physics, not your aim going bad. (It's also why we make both SWIFT and CONTROL.)

SENSITIVITY

Find Your eDPI

eDPI = mouse DPI × in-game sensitivity — the only fair way to compare settings across players. Most Valorant pros sit around 160–400 eDPI; CS2 pros roughly 600–1000.

Low eDPI means big arm swipes, and big swipes need desk space: 450mm wide at minimum, or a full desk mat. If your pad keeps ending mid-flick, your sens isn't wrong — your surface is too small.

SWITCHES

Optical vs Mechanical Mouse Switches

Optical switches fire with a beam of light: near-zero debounce delay and no contact wear, so double-click faults are rare. Mechanical switches offer more variety in feel and sound, at a few milliseconds more latency.

In practice: if you play competitive shooters, optical removes one failure mode from your life. If you love a specific click feel, mechanical still has the bigger menu.
